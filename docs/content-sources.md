# Dream DNA Content Sources

The waiting facts are editorial context, not medical advice, diagnosis, or a claim that a specific dream has a fixed meaning. The application stores paraphrased facts in the database rather than fetching web content at runtime.

## Cognitive Analysis Fact Category

The `cognitiveFacts` bank is grounded in these reference sources:

- [NINDS, Understanding Sleep](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/understanding-sleep.pdf): sleep stages, dreaming, and the limits of dream-purpose claims.
- [NIH News in Health, Sleep On It](https://newsinhealth.nih.gov/2013/04/sleep-it): sleep, learning, memory, and the hippocampus.
- [NIH Common Fund, Exploring Dreams and Memory](https://commonfund.nih.gov/pioneer/highlights/exploring-dreams-and-memory): memory reorganization during sleep and the distinction between memory processing and conscious dream stories.
- [APA Dictionary of Psychology, reconstructive memory](https://dictionary.apa.org/reconstructive-memory): the reconstructive nature of recall.
- [Diekelmann, Wilhelm, and Born, *Sleep Medicine Reviews*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19251443/): review of sleep-dependent memory consolidation.

The seed bank is deterministic by date and creates three facts per day for the next thirteen months. It is reviewed as product content when the source list or wording changes.

## Dream Map Research Lenses

Pattern details use cautious research lenses rather than a universal symbol dictionary. The API attaches the source title, publication year, and URL to every displayed lens.

- [Schredl and Hofmann, *Continuity between waking activities and dream activities*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12763010/): supports exploring emotional involvement and waking-life continuity while acknowledging that incorporation varies.
- [Malinowski and Horton, *Memory sources of dreams*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24635722/): supports treating people, places, and objects as potentially fragmented autobiographical material rather than literal predictions.
- [Fogli et al., *Our dreams, our selves*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32968499/): supports descriptive comparison of recurring characters, interactions, and emotions across dream reports.

These lenses are informational and non-diagnostic. Personalized pattern reflections are separately generated from the user's own encrypted journal synthesis and must cite only extracted pattern evidence.
